# Storytelling principles — Amberbrew Story

**Status:** Agreed creative direction, documented on 2026-11-01 after a ten-question discussion. This is a **reference for future writing**, not a story draft or permission to invent character canon.

The **human creative** chose the principles below. AI asked clarifying questions and helped consolidate the answers into editorial guidance. Illustrative suggestions made during that discussion are not independent confirmation that an event occurred.

## 1. Learn about characters by experiencing them

Use an observant narrative perspective. Let readers discover the Amberbrews through actions, words, gestures, habits, relationships, silences and ordinary routines. Occasional inner thoughts are possible, but do not expose everyone's full private emotional life. Partial knowledge makes the characters feel like people with lives of their own.

## 2. Grounded prose with gentle poetry

Keep language simple, direct, believable and carefully observed. A light lyrical current, sensory detail and modest poetic imagery are welcome, provided they do not drown out clarity or authenticity. The creative expressed a preference for slightly more lyricism than plain description, **not a literal percentage-based formula**.

Avoid extravagant declarations, constant metaphors, overwrought emotion and overstimulation. Respect the physical setting: for example, **do not invent rain falling in Ironforge**. Do not assume that the family's busy tavern must close at a convenient story beat.

## 3. Adventure moves; everyday life deepens

Actual World of Warcraft gameplay and roleplaying will often supply adventure and progression. Intentionally give more room to family life, cooking, brewing, gatherings, work, friendships and quiet returns home. These are meaningful material, not filler added between action scenes.

## 4. Imagination fills spaces without rewriting history

Believable smaller incidents, conversations and gestures may be imagined around real gameplay and established character history. That freedom stops where it would contradict confirmed facts. A written scene is **not automatically** a literal gameplay record or permanent character canon.

## 5. A loving but imperfect family

The Amberbrews may be eccentric, absent-minded, stubborn, overly busy or drawn to unusual interests. Their foundation remains kindness, loyalty, affection and mutual support. Gentle misunderstandings and familiar flaws can exist, but sustained bitterness, heavy domestic conflict or manufactured tragedy should not define the project.

## 6. Humor without performance

Humor should emerge naturally from personalities and situations: affectionate teasing, small ironies, dry observations, laughter and familiar habits. These are **not comedy stories**. Do not manufacture punchlines, spectacular embarrassment or relentless drunken absurdity.

## 7. Let moments breathe

Allow a scene to linger in gestures, pauses, details and atmosphere even if no major event occurs. The user-led gameplay sessions already supply movement over time; a fictional account need not hurry from event to event or impose a strong dramatic arc.

## 8. Keep mystery genuinely unresolved

Unexplained possessions, habits, overheard fragments and private histories may remain unknown forever. **A mystery is not a promise of a twist, a reveal or a future story arc.** Do not answer questions merely because they have been raised.

## 9. Respect Warcraft as a welcoming foundation

Work within established Warcraft history, cultures and geography, developing original Amberbrew details in its less-defined spaces. Their unusual good-hearted Dark Iron household is intentional. Acknowledge that **World of Warcraft Forever's continuity may evolve**; future official or server-specific changes should be checked before a supposed contradiction is resolved.

Distinguish established setting information from original family lore. Do not turn characters into tour guides explaining their own world.

## 10. Offer a window into lives already in motion

The intended reader feels like a welcome observer, catching only part of a family whose daily life continues off the page. The image of a small window near the hidden Ironforge tavern communicates this experience. Warmth, faintly overheard conversations and things left out of sight should invite **curious wonder**, not force an emotional lesson.

The image is an **editorial metaphor**, not definitive evidence of the building's floor plan. Food, drink and household details mentioned as examples are also not automatically established historical events.

## Practical boundaries

- Use [CANON.md](CANON.md), [TIMELINE.md](TIMELINE.md) and [continuity notes](lore/continuity-notes.md) for current family facts.
- Record actual or remembered sessions in [journal/](journal/) with source and uncertainty. Place imagined narratives in [stories/](stories/).
- Do not turn an assistant's example, draft or invention into accepted canon without a creative decision.
- Do not force a moral, conflict, climax, revelation, redemption arc or conventional ending.
- Character dialogue should sound individual and natural, not uniformly theatrical or faux-archaic.
- Keep private information, unpublished conversations and the difference between gameplay and fiction respected.

## Public-facing project documentation is different

The prose rules above govern **possible future in-world stories**. The development journal, concept and other project-history documents speak from the **human creative's first-person perspective**, while never beginning a sentence with “I” or “My”. They explain the reason for the work, what the creative decided, the AI's actual contribution and the result.

**Current work is documentation only. No story, scene, invented incident or new character biography is authorized by this guide.**
