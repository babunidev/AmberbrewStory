# The Warm Room

There was a part of the evening when the brewery grew too warm for anyone to pretend they were cold.

The heat came up from the kettles and out of the kitchen, from the crowded benches and the stove that had been going since morning. Beneath the Bronze Kettle, in the Amberbrews' place, a door opened somewhere beyond the room and let in a brief, welcome current of cooler air. Three customers turned toward it. None of them got up.

Bess Amberbrew carried a great platter of beer-basted boar ribs against one hip.

"Mind your elbows," she said.

A man at the nearest table drew his mug in so quickly that it struck his teeth. Bess laughed, and her laughter filled the room long after she had put the platter down.

"That's better. Now eat before somebody begins telling me how hungry he is."

"Already told you," the man said.

"And I already fed you."

At the worktable, Buxbrew was attempting to separate a bundle of herbs from the string in which she had tied them. Her copper-beaded braids kept falling into her eyes. Every time she tucked one back, the other found its way forward.

Bess watched for a moment.

"A knife," she said.

"There's no need."

The string tightened beneath Buxbrew's fingers.

"Of course not."

A little later, Buxbrew fetched a knife.

The kitchen smelled of roasted fat, malt and the sharp, green things drying above the shelves. Buxbrew liked the green smell best when the fire was low and the pots had stopped boiling. She rubbed one leaf between her fingers, considered it, and put it apart from the others. There was always another small experiment to make. Some were for the cooking. Some were for the bottles she kept well away from anything meant to be drunk with supper.

Bess set a mug of Rhapsody Malt beside her.

"Something to go with all that thinking."

"I'm working."

"Then something to go with all that working."

Buxbrew raised the mug in acknowledgment. "Cheers."

From the benches came the scrape of a chair and a burst of argument about a hunting story. A customer had apparently killed the same bear three different ways. His companion objected to all three. Bess turned her head without moving from the worktable.

"If there's a fourth way, save it for tomorrow."

The two men quieted, though not because they had settled anything.

Morkah sat near the farther wall with a cup that had gone untouched for some time. The light reached the edge of his beard and one hand resting on the table. He listened to the room without seeming to attend to any single conversation.

Buxbrew held out a sprig of the herb she had separated.

"Do you know this one?"

He looked at it, then at the little heap on the table.

"Know what you're putting it in?"

"Not yet."

"Then I wouldn't hurry."

He returned to his cup.

Buxbrew put the sprig down. It might have been advice about herbs. With Morkah, there was seldom a convenient way of knowing.

Later, someone started keeping time by knocking a spoon against a mug. Two people joined in with their palms. Buxbrew looked up from the worktable. Bess saw the look and moved the nearest full tankard out of the way before her daughter had even stood.

"Mind the boards," Bess said.

"They've held before."

"That's what worries me."

Buxbrew climbed onto the table, steady on her thick boots, and began to dance. The room gave her a rough, affectionate rhythm. One of the men who had argued over the bear stamped badly out of time. His companion informed him of this. Neither stopped smiling.

Bess gathered the empty dishes. She was still gathering them when Buxbrew came down again, breathless and hungry, and discovered that her mother had kept a portion of the ribs warm.

"Wasn't sure you'd leave me any," Buxbrew said.

Bess set the plate before her.

"A house where you can't get supper after dancing isn't much of a house."

The evening went on. Someone came in. Someone else went out carrying a wrapped parcel of food. The kettles cooled slowly, the herbs waited on their string, and at the far table Morkah finally lifted his cup.

Whatever had been troubling the hunter was still troubling him. The bear had not been settled either. There was time enough for both tomorrow.
