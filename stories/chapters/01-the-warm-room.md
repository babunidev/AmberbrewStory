# The Warm Room

The room was always warmest by the end of the evening.

Heat rose from the brewery's working kettles and lingered above the crowded benches. It settled into the wood of the tables, into the dark stone beneath the Bronze Kettle, and into the clothes of those who had stayed for a second drink and then a third. Above the kitchen door, bundles of drying herbs brushed the lintel whenever someone passed carrying a full tray.

Bess Amberbrew had spent much of the afternoon among the ovens. Now she moved through the guests with a broad platter of beer-basted boar ribs resting against her hip. She knew the crowded passage between the tables by heart. A customer withdrew his elbow just as she approached; another had the sense to pull his mug aside.

"Mind yourselves," she said, and her voice carried from the hearth to the far wall.

A few heads turned, though no one seemed surprised. Bess could make herself heard through the noise of a full room without sounding as if she were calling for anyone in particular.

Her daughter was at the long worktable, sorting herbs by the light of a low lamp. Jasmine had taken off her outer coat and rolled her sleeves back. Her copper-beaded braids kept falling forward when she bent over the leaves. Beside the bundles lay a narrow knife, several small jars and a piece of paper marked with measurements she had amended twice.

She separated a brittle stem from the others and held it against the light. The scent was clean and slightly bitter. She rubbed the leaf between two fingers and set it aside, then reached for a fresh sprig. There was no reason to finish quickly. Most of the mixtures she wanted to try could wait until morning.

A mug appeared beside her elbow.

"Working still?" Bess asked.

"Only sorting."

"Then you're not too busy to drink."

The mug held Rhapsody Malt. Jasmine smiled and moved her paper farther from it.

Across the room, a man with a beard full of breadcrumbs had begun telling the story of a bear he had encountered on the road. Two of his companions supplied corrections. Their disagreement grew steadily more confident, although none appeared to have been present for the same part of the adventure.

Bess returned to the kitchen with an empty platter. The door swung open and released a little cloud of steam. For a moment the sounds of chopping, water running and spoons striking pans mingled with the conversation outside.

Morkah sat where the firelight reached the edge of his table. A cup rested near his hand. He had been listening for some time to the men arguing about the bear, but when one appealed to him for judgment, he only looked down into his drink.

"Didn't see the creature," he said.

The men resumed their argument without his assistance.

Jasmine carried her sorted herbs to the shelves. The old wood had darkened where generations of wet hands had touched it; beneath the jars lay a dusting of flour that no one seemed able to keep away. She found a place for the last bundle and turned to discover that somebody had begun beating time with a spoon against a mug.

A second rhythm joined it, then the tapping of boots beneath the tables. It was not quite a song yet. Someone tried to sing anyway.

"Buxbrew!" called one of the guests. "You'll not leave us to make all the music."

She looked toward the table, then toward her mother. Bess had already moved a tankard away from the edge.

"Mind the boards," Bess said.

Jasmine climbed up with the easy balance of someone accustomed to much worse footing. The boards were firm beneath her boots. She danced while the company kept imperfect time, her thick braids shifting against her shoulders, laughter rising whenever the rhythm escaped them.

It did not last long. There were dishes to clear and more guests coming in. By the time she stepped down, Bess had saved her a plate of ribs beside the hearth.

They ate at the corner of the worktable when they could find the space. Warm grease shone on the meat, and the bread was soft enough to tear apart with one hand. From the back room came the steady settling sounds of the brewery at the end of a long day: a lid placed on a vessel, a bucket being moved, someone sweeping beneath a bench.

The man with the breadcrumbs was still discussing the bear. His companions had found another way to disagree about it.

Morkah lifted his cup. Bess called for a clean plate. Jasmine took the last piece of bread and used it to gather the gravy. Outside the little circle of lamplight, the work went on, and the room was warm.
