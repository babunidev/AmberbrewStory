# Keep the Fire

By the time evening found Loch Modan again, her boots had dried.

She had spent most of the day in the loose way of someone who had not slept properly: eating when food appeared, sitting longer than she meant to, walking until a place looked quiet enough to stop. By dusk she had come back to the water. The loch lay dark between the hills, with the last of the light resting on it in a narrow silver band.

Jasmine made a small fire above the shore.

It was not much. Fallen wood, dry grass, a few stubborn sticks that smoked before they caught. She crouched over it with both hands out until the warmth reached her knuckles. Once the flames settled, she stopped doing anything at all.

Fire was easy to watch.

At home, fire had always been busy. The Great Forge had work to do. The kitchen fires had pots above them, bread near them, somebody reaching past somebody else for a spoon. Even a brewery flame belonged to a kettle and a task.

This one had nothing to prove.

It crackled softly among the stones while the loch moved beyond it. She watched a thin twig burn bright at one end, bend, and fall through the coals.

*Stay.*

She looked over her shoulder.

There was nobody on the shore.

A little farther up the bank, the road was empty. No boots on stone. No shape between the trees. No one crouched on the other side of the fire playing a joke.

She turned back slowly.

The word had not crossed the air. It had arrived closer than that, somewhere behind hearing and before thought. Yet it had not felt like one of her own thoughts either.

For a while, nothing else came.

She poked at the fire with a stick.

*Stay at the fire.*

The voice was warm. Not loud, not commanding. It seemed in no hurry at all.

Jasmine rested the stick across her knees.

“And who are you, then?”

*Eldgrim.*

The name came without explanation.

She waited for one.

None followed.

“Never heard of you.”

There was a pause long enough for a small flame to catch on the bark of a fresh branch.

*That is all right.*

She gave a short laugh through her nose.

“Good.”

The loch lapped against the stones below.

*Keep the fire.*

Jasmine glanced down at it. “Seems to be keeping itself.”

*Then keep it company.*

That earned another little laugh.

She stayed.

Minutes passed. Perhaps longer. The strange part was how quickly the strangeness stopped being the largest thing in the evening. The fire was still warm. Her feet still ached. Somewhere across the water, something called once from the dark and went quiet again.

“What do you want?”

The answer did not come at once.

*There are others who stop for the flames.*

She looked into the fire.

“Here?”

*Elsewhere.*

Another branch settled with a shower of sparks.

*Find them.*

Jasmine rubbed a thumb over one bruised knuckle.

“And what am I meant to do when I do?”

*Make room.*

The words sat with her more easily than they should have.

She thought of crowded tables beneath Ironforge. Of Bess finding another plate when there were already too many elbows at the table. Of mugs pushed sideways, benches shifted, somebody arriving late and being fed before they had properly said hello.

The voice returned, as gentle as before.

*Let them bring a story if they have one. Let them bring silence if they do not.*

Jasmine watched the coals glow beneath the wood.

“All right.”

Nothing grand happened after that.

No flame leapt higher. No mark appeared in the stones. The loch remained the loch, black and broad beneath the night.

She added another branch.

Much later, when the fire had burned low enough that she could feel the cold returning through her sleeves, the voice came once more.

*Keep the fire.*

Then there was only the water.

Jasmine stayed until the last branch broke into coals.

After that evening, she began to notice fires differently.

A cookfire beside a road was no longer only smoke and supper. A brazier outside an inn was worth slowing for. When travelers stood near a flame after their meal was finished, she noticed which of them were in no hurry to leave.

At first she only spoke to them.

A greeting. A question about the road. Sometimes a story. Sometimes nothing more than sharing the warmth for a few minutes before going separate ways.

It was enough to begin with.

By the time the road brought her back toward Kharanos, she had started asking names.
